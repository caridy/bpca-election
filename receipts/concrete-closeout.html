After years of complex restoration, this program is in final-mile punchout and permit-closeout execution.

Concrete restoration and recertification scope had been open for years, with high disruption and limited confidence about finish timelines. This board kept the effort moving across multiple contractors and continued coordination with ACG, the engineering firm that has been involved in this cycle for more than eight years.

Where We Are Now

The latest engineer milestone report documents the punchout list required to complete the structural report package. Many of those items are already completed or partially completed, and the balance is expected to be closed in the coming months.

Engineer Remaining-Items List (SD6, as of January 19, 2026)

  • Four tower columns (one at a time): shoring installation, removal of damaged sections, placement of new concrete, and shoring removal.
  • Pool deck repairs: three garage-level columns and final deck repairs.
  • South side garage deck section (see SD-6 plan): nine slab sections, with potential joist and small beam repairs.
  • North side garage deck section (4-5 bays each direction): fresh ACG survey, mostly slab sections, plus at least two joist/beam repairs.

Since that January milestone snapshot, multiple listed items have already moved to completed or partially completed status, with the remainder on an active closeout path.

Execution Model

Methodical closeout: this has been a multi-year, high-cost, high-complexity effort managed without losing continuity.

Monumental coordination effort: more than 100 meetings with contractors, construction committee participants, management, invited experts, peer review, and owners were required to keep the work aligned and moving.

Surgical phasing: work was sequenced so residents could continue daily life while structural obligations advanced toward completion.

Engineering continuity: long-term coordination with ACG and project partners kept the technical path consistent through changing requirements and field conditions.

Why This Matters

This is foundational risk-reduction work, not cosmetic work. Completing this cycle moves the community from extended structural recovery into a planned-value phase, with approximately ten years to prepare and improve before the 60-year recertification cycle.
