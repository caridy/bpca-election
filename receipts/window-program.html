Window Program Mobilization

A multi-year IRMA insurance recovery effort converted into an active, owner-driven bulk replacement program with structured financing, professional inspection oversight, and 300+ participating units.

What We Inherited

Hurricane IRMA caused widespread window damage across the property. The board pursued insurance recovery, secured settlement funds, and faced a decision: distribute the money to individual owners for ad-hoc replacements, or convert the recovery into a coordinated bulk program with economies of scale, quality control, and long-term value protection.

The board chose the bulk program path, requiring years of vendor evaluation, specification development, financing structuring, and owner opt-in coordination.

What Was Delivered

IRMA insurance recovery: The board pursued and secured insurance settlement funds for hurricane-damaged windows, then structured a path to convert individual payouts into a coordinated bulk program.

Vendor selection and role definition (competitive process): After extensive evaluation and a competitive bid process that began with 18 window contractors, the board formalized a three-vendor structure: ESWindows for manufacturing, Windready for installation, and Miami Curtain Wall Corporation (MCWC) for independent inspection and quality oversight.

Specification and inspection framework: Working with MCWC, the board developed technical specifications, inspection protocols, and quality standards to ensure consistent installation across hundreds of units.

Owner opt-in coordination: Over 300 units opted into the program through a structured JotForm mechanism, with clear opt-in/opt-out rules, pricing transparency via SharePoint, and recurring webinars to address owner questions.

Financing structure: The board is negotiating financing options with banks to offer owners flexible payment paths: one-time payment, 6-month plan, or 6.5-year financing at approximately 6-7% interest.

Opt-out restrictions and compliance: Owners who opted out face clear restrictions: no individual window replacement until the bulk program concludes, post-project compliance with MCWC specifications and inspection requirements, and potential mandatory replacement by end of 2027 per MCWC inspection guidance.

Phased rollout preparation: Sample unit installations were staged in early 2026, mock-ups are underway (February 2026), the master permit is under City review and nearing approval (expected March 2026 / Q1 2026), and bulk material ordering is expected after mock-up approval.

Webinar cadence and transparency: The board maintained recurring webinars throughout 2024-2026 to communicate pricing, timelines, financing options, and program logistics, with a February 17, 2026 webinar specifically referenced in the latest newsletter.

The program is in pre-commencement phase: mock-ups are underway (February 2026), the master permit is under City review and nearing approval (expected March 2026 / Q1 2026), and bulk material ordering is expected after mock-up approval. Over 300 units are committed to the program, with financing and payment mechanisms finalized.

Why This Matters

Converting an insurance recovery into a coordinated bulk program required years of planning, vendor negotiation, financing structuring, and owner coordination. The alternative—distributing insurance funds for ad-hoc individual replacements—would have resulted in inconsistent quality, no economies of scale, no inspection oversight, and potential long-term building envelope problems.

The bulk program delivers professional manufacturing, certified installation, independent inspection, structured financing, and consistent specifications across 300+ units. This protects property values, ensures building envelope integrity, and provides owners with financing flexibility that individual replacements would not offer.

The opt-out restrictions are not punitive—they're protective. Allowing mixed individual replacements during the bulk program would create coordination chaos, compromise building envelope consistency, and undermine the quality control framework that makes the program valuable.

They also reduce risk. Allowing mixed individual replacements during a bulk program can create a dangerous scenario if expedited protection of exposed window openings becomes necessary during hurricane seasons.
