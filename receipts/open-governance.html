Open-Access Governance

A 582-unit association operating with unprecedented transparency: 40+ board meetings in 5 years, extensive committee work, recorded sessions, and multiple direct communication channels.

What We Inherited

Many associations operate at the statutory minimum: one board meeting per year, limited owner access, and minimal documentation. For a 582-unit property managing complex, multi-million dollar projects, this baseline would have been insufficient.

40+ Board meetings in 5 years (statute requires only 1 annually)
Dozens Committee meetings across finance, construction, beautification, and grievance
6+ Townhalls and webinars for high-impact projects in the last 2 years
Recorded Board meetings now recorded for owner review
12+ Bi-monthly newsletters with detailed progress updates over 2 years

What Was Delivered

Board meeting frequency: Over 40 board meetings in 5 years—far exceeding the statutory requirement of one annual meeting—to maintain transparency during complex project execution.

Active committee structure: Dozens of committee meetings across finance, construction, beautification, and grievance committees, providing peer review and owner input on major decisions.

Bi-monthly newsletters: More than a dozen newsletters published over the last 2 years with extensive project progress details, available in BuildingLink under Official Documents → Communications → Newsletters.

Townhalls and webinars: Six+ townhalls and webinars held in the last 2 years for high-impact projects, with board meetings now recorded for owners who cannot attend live.

No question left unanswered: Board meetings consistently addressed every owner question, with substantive responses rather than deflection or deferral.

Unprecedented documentation access: A comprehensive library of documents made available through BuildingLink—board packets, meeting minutes, engineering reports, contractor agreements, and project updates. The volume has become so extensive it presents its own navigation challenge.

Modern communication channels: WhatsApp Home number introduced for fast, reliable resident-to-staff communication. Board members remain accessible through the Amigos WhatsApp Group, answering questions and receiving feedback directly from owners.

Continuity with rotation: Multiple board members rotated through service over 5 years while maintaining project momentum and institutional knowledge.

How Owners Can Verify

  • BuildingLink document library: Board Meeting Packets folder showing 40+ meetings over 5 years.
  • Recorded board meetings and townhall/webinar archives.
  • WhatsApp Home number and Amigos WhatsApp Group activity logs.
  • Published owner updates, project communications, and engineering reports in BuildingLink.
  • Historical board service records showing member rotation and continuity.

Why This Matters

For a 582-unit association, this level of communication and documentation is unprecedented. Most properties this size operate at statutory minimums—one meeting per year, limited owner access, minimal documentation.

This board chose a different model: high-frequency meetings, active committees, recorded sessions, comprehensive documentation, and direct communication channels. The result is a governance structure that reduces rumor cycles, improves execution quality, and builds owner confidence during years of complex, high-stakes project work.

The accessibility came at a cost—board members fielded questions at all hours, committee volunteers invested significant time, and the documentation volume became a management challenge—but it created the transparency foundation that allowed $20M+ in special assessment work to proceed with owner understanding and support.
