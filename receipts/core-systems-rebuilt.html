Core Systems Rebuilt

A methodical, multi-year infrastructure rebuild delivered modern mechanical systems, rebuilt reserves, and long-term operational reliability without disrupting daily life.

What We Inherited

For over a decade, the association collected only 11% of the operational budget for reserves—the legal minimum—while keeping HOA fees frozen. This chronic underfunding left critical infrastructure aging without replacement capital, ultimately requiring a nearly $20 million special assessment to address deferred maintenance and system failures.

The reserve study had not been updated in over 10 years, and core mechanical systems—valves, pumps, generators, and supporting infrastructure—were operating well past their expected service life.

What Was Delivered

Reserve study modernization: The reserve study was updated in 2025 to reflect current replacement costs, system conditions, and realistic funding requirements after more than a decade without updates.

Reserve rebuilding discipline: After years of minimum-compliance reserve funding, the board shifted to a sustainable reserve accumulation strategy to reduce future special assessment risk.

Systematic infrastructure replacement: Nearly every critical mechanical system was rebuilt over multiple years—methodically, piece by piece—to avoid catastrophic failures and minimize disruption to residents' daily lives.

2025 valve and pump replacement: All main valves across every water line were replaced, along with all pumps in both towers, modernizing the core water distribution and pressure systems.

Generator, mechanical room, and supporting equipment upgrades: High-risk infrastructure components received major repair or full replacement to improve emergency preparedness and operational reliability.

Domestic backflow preventer replacement (Building B): The domestic backflow preventer was fully replaced in 2025 after the original unit exceeded its useful life and became non-repairable. This upgrade protects the county water supply during pressure-loss events by preventing reverse flow contamination.

Why This Matters

This was not a single project—it was a multi-year recovery effort to undo more than a decade of deferred maintenance and reserve underfunding. The result is a property with modernized core systems, reduced emergency repair risk, and a sustainable reserve funding model that protects owners from future assessment shocks.

The 2025 valve and pump replacement alone represents the kind of foundational work that most owners never see but directly impacts water pressure, system reliability, and long-term building integrity. Completing this work methodically, without extended service disruptions, required careful planning and execution discipline.
