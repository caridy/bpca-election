Frequently Asked Questions

Direct answers to common questions about projects, timelines, finances, and operations.

When will the 40-year recertification be complete?
The structural work is in final closeout phase. The engineering firm is completing the punchout list before submitting the certification report to the City of Miami. Final timing depends on contractor completion of remaining items and engineering sign-off. Current status is available in the Concrete Closeout details.
Are there any additional special assessments planned?
No. The association now operates with surplus budgets and fully funded reserves. The major special assessment cycle is complete, and the financial foundation is stable enough to handle ongoing operations and planned capital work without requiring additional owner assessments.
How is the office improving response times to owner requests?
Management is implementing better workflow systems, including AI-assisted request filtering and prioritization. The focus has shifted to faster initial responses rather than delaying until complete answers are ready. WhatsApp is expanding for urgent issues, with security now handling after-hours matters. It's an active area of improvement given the high volume across multiple channels.
Why does the window program take so long?
This isn't a simple window swap—it's a 300+ unit engineering project with 200-page specifications. The scope includes redesigned breakmetal systems to prevent future water intrusion and concrete damage. Replacing one window is straightforward, but 300+ requires extensive permitting, multi-crew coordination across towers, and strict separation of IRMA insurance funds (awarded for 100 units, covering partial costs). Every dollar and detail must be tracked separately. Scale changes everything.
Why does St. Regis construction seem faster than our 40-year work?
St. Regis is ground-up construction on a cleared lot with multi-shift crews working extended hours under city variances. Brickell Place is restoration in an occupied building—structural repairs must be phased around residents, regulatory compliance is more complex, and work can't disrupt normal living. Comparing new construction speed to occupied restoration isn't apples-to-apples.
What governance improvements were made under this board?
The board runs 40+ meetings per year versus the statutory minimum of one. Committees meet regularly, townhalls and webinars address major projects, meetings are now recorded, and BuildingLink hosts an extensive document library. Bi-monthly newsletters, WhatsApp channels, and direct board access create transparency that's rare for a 582-unit association. Questions don't go unanswered.
Will HOA fees increase?
Brickell Place maintains the lowest HOA fees in the neighborhood while operating at surplus. Disciplined financial management and operational efficiency have kept fees stable and competitive. There are no current plans for increases beyond normal inflationary adjustments that all buildings face.
How do I report building issues or security concerns?
WhatsApp has become the primary channel for urgent matters, with security handling after-hours responses. For non-urgent issues, use BuildingLink work orders, email, or phone. The office is actively improving response workflows across all channels to handle the high request volume more efficiently.
What's the plan after concrete and pool work finishes?
The strategy is exterior-first: pool deck, common outdoor areas, and visible shared spaces where improvements benefit everyone immediately. Then interior phasing—hallways, lobbies, and underutilized spaces. The approach is methodical and value-focused, not reactive. Every decision prioritizes livability and property value.
How was delinquency reduced so dramatically?
Consistent enforcement, clear communication about consequences, and payment plan options for owners facing hardship. The spike during the special assessment was addressed through structured collection processes and legal action when necessary. Dropping from 10% to under 1.5% required discipline and follow-through, not just policy.
What happens if I opted out of the window program?
Opt-out owners cannot replace windows individually until the bulk program completes. Any future replacement must meet MCWC specifications, pass inspection requirements, and may be mandated by late 2027 per engineering guidance. Opting out delays the work but doesn't eliminate the eventual requirement.
How can I participate or ask more questions?
Join the Amigos WhatsApp Group (200+ owners), attend board meetings (schedules in BuildingLink), review meeting packets and newsletters, or contact board members directly. The next phase needs creative input from owners with design, construction, and planning skills. Participation is how good ideas become real improvements.
